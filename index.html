How the peg pyramid works

The board is built as a triangle of staggered pegs, with each row wider than the one above it. A ball released at the top can move left or right at every peg, and that sequence repeats until it reaches the bottom. The final landing place is one of the pockets lined up along the base — always one more pocket than there are rows.

Because every bounce is a binary split, most balls drift toward the centre, while edge pockets appear far less often. Mathematicians know this structure as a Galton board, and the result follows a binomial distribution. In practice, the payout table mirrors that curve: central pockets pay less often but more commonly, while outer pockets pay more but hit far less often.

Multipliers, odds, and the house margin

Each pocket multiplier is tied to the chance of reaching it, then trimmed slightly by the house margin. If you compare the probability of each pocket with its payout, the total lands just below one, which is how the operator keeps a long-term edge. Published return-to-player figures for the game usually sit somewhere around 97% to 99%, depending on the studio and the selected settings.

The main point is simple: no row count or risk level changes the long-term maths in the player’s favour. The settings only move variance around, so the ride feels different even when the underlying return stays much the same.

Straight answers to five questions

Is every drop genuinely random?

On a licensed platform, yes. Each deflection is handled by an audited random number generator, and provably fair systems let you verify that the result was fixed before the ball moved.

Do more rows make the game pay better?

No. Extra rows add more pockets and stretch the odds curve, so edge payouts get larger while their chance of landing gets smaller. The overall return remains broadly the same.

Which risk level is the smart choice?

There is no mathematically best setting. Low risk smooths out the swings, while high risk shifts more value to rare edge hits. The right choice depends on your budget and your tolerance for volatility.
